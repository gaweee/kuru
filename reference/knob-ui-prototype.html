Waking up

From sleep, the last known state shows straight away in muted colours while ticks scan for Wi-Fi, then Home Assistant. When fresh state arrives a wave lights the ring, the inner line and ripples draw back in, and anything that changed while it slept counts to its new value.

    How it behaves

    Wake
    When the screen is dimmed or off, the first turn or tap only brings it back, so nothing changes by accident. The ring sweeps up to each device’s current state while it syncs.
    Sync
    Waking shows the last known state at once, muted, with the inner line and ripples hidden because the real state isn’t known yet. Status words only appear if a step takes longer than 250 ms, so a fast wake is just the scan and the wave. Turns made before it connects are queued and sent once it does.
    Offline
    If Wi-Fi or Home Assistant can’t be reached, it keeps showing the last known state, says which one failed, and retries every 4 s.
    Read
    Lit ticks are what you’re asking for. The thin inner line, and the ripples or curtain pleats behind the numbers, are what Home Assistant reports. When they meet, the room has caught up.
    Adjust
    One detent, one step, one light haptic tick. Spin fast on lights or curtains and steps grow to 2× and 3×. Fan speeds move one at a time with a firmer tick.
    Send
    The number updates live. After 0.6 s of stillness one command goes out. Leaving a device sends any unsent change straight away.
    Limits
    At either end the knob bumps and the ring nudges back. Aircon stops at 16°; turn once more to switch it off. From off, one click up resumes the last setpoint.
    Failure
    If Home Assistant doesn’t confirm within 2.5 s, the value springs back to the real state and the screen says why.
    Sleep
    Dims after 20 s without input. Screen off at 30 s.

    Notes for the build

    262K colours is 18-bit, and LVGL will most likely drive the panel at 16-bit RGB565: 32 levels of red and blue, 64 of green. Smooth dark gradients band badly at that depth, so the screen has none. Every colour is a flat value picked from the 16-bit palette, and light is drawn as ripple lines and pleats rather than glows. Use the colour depth switch under the knob to check.

    Very dark warm tints collapse to grey at 16-bit. The background, #181410, sits exactly on 16-bit values and keeps its warmth. The panel is IPS, so black still glows a little; a warm charcoal makes that look intentional.

    Waveshare’s parts list shows the encoder, a power button and a BOOT button, but no push switch on the knob. Press therefore maps to a tap on the glass, which sits in the middle of the knob anyway.

    Haptics run through the onboard DRV2605. The effect numbers under the knob are suggestions from its ROM library; confirm them on the real motor. If the encoder has physical clicks, match one software step to each click.

    Everything is flat arcs, lines, circles and text, which LVGL draws cheaply and which can’t band. Dim with the backlight PWM rather than by drawing darker colours. Subset the numeral font to digits and a few symbols.

    Keep the last known state in RAM through light sleep so the first frame needs no network. Keeping Wi-Fi associated during light sleep makes waking mostly a state fetch; switching it off saves battery but adds association and DHCP time, which a static IP and a saved channel help shorten. The timings in this mockup are rough placeholders, so measure them on the board.

    The scan and the wave only recolour a handful of ticks per frame, so LVGL redraws small regions and the animation stays smooth over QSPI.
